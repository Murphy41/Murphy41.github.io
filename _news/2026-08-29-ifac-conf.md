In August, I attended the **IFAC World Congress 2026** in **Busan, South Korea**, where I was very happy to present our work, **"Motor Cost Re-Optimization in Indirect Human Movement Pattern Adaptation."**

This work continues a research thread from our earlier [**IEEE TNSRE paper, "Inducing Human Motor Adaptation Without Explicit Error Feedback: A Motor Cost Approach"**](https://doi.org/10.1109/TNSRE.2021.3096516). In that study, we showed that human movement patterns could be reshaped implicitly by altering motor cost, without explicit movement errors or instructions.

At IFAC, we revisited the same experimental data from a more quantitative perspective to better understand what was driving that adaptation. By **estimating and decomposing the motor cost**, we found that the observed adaptation was primarily associated with reducing the artificial load introduced by the robotic intervention. The results also highlighted that people may adopt different strategies when responding to the same altered cost landscape.

Beyond the presentation, IFAC was also a memorable week of plenary talks, catching up with friends and researchers across the control community, and, of course, the **Control Orchestra** at the closing ceremony.
